# Draft first message to the client

**Subject:** Six questions that change who we recommend

Hello,

I have built a working matcher on the sample you sent. Before results are relied on, six decisions are yours, because each one changes who is recommended. For each I have taken a default, shown in brackets, and the tool can switch it in settings.

1. **Notes ahead of the vendor feed.** The account manager's note of 5 November relaxes the home-health job to one year of experience with Homecare Homebase trainable; the feed still says two years, required. May a recruiter act on the note before the feed is updated, and would a submission be rejected meanwhile? *(I use the note, flag every result that depends on it, and ask for confirmation before submitting. With the note, ten people qualify; with the feed alone, none.)*

2. **Which source wins when two disagree.** Your discovery notes list source precedence as undecided. For job fields and candidate facts, should the newest statement win, or should a named source be the authority (for example the vendor feed for start dates)? *(Newest wins; the older one stays visible.)*

3. **What "minimum experience" means.** Years in the job's care setting, or in the profession? *(In the setting. Otherwise a ten-year outpatient therapist qualifies for home health.)*

4. **Claims without dates.** Does a candidate's own statement such as "three years of home-health work" count toward a minimum when their dated history shows less? *(Only dated history counts; the claim is shown and becomes the question to ask.)*

5. **No-contact requests.** When a candidate asks not to be contacted, does that apply at once, or only once a recruiter updates the record? *(At once. Two candidates in the sample asked; one record still says contact is allowed and the other's restriction field is empty.)*

6. **How many people each job needs.** No record states openings or a submission limit. How many does each job need, and how many may be submitted? *(The list is not cut off; it opens on the top ten.)*

Two smaller requests would make the scoring better grounded: a history of submissions and placements, so the weights can be calibrated per division rather than set by judgement; and a decision on whether candidate text may leave your systems, which decides whether a language model may help read notes.

Thank you,
Sukriti
